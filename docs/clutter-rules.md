# What counts as clutter

The rules for judging one photo: what the clutter suggestions may offer for the trash, and how labels on
the developer page (`dev-clutter.html`, see [clutter-lab.md](clutter-lab.md)) are settled. They are for
**most people's** photos, not one library: the list ships to everyone, so it must not learn one person's
taste. A draft, to be refined as labelling shows where it is unclear.

## The principle

**Offer only what almost anyone would call clutter.** Every user reviews each suggestion and unticks what
they keep, so the list doesn't have to match anyone's taste exactly; it has to stay out of the grey zone.

That makes judging lopsided, on purpose:

- **Kept by me, clutter to most people** (a nice café photo): fine to offer. I untick it; little harm.
- **Clutter to me, kept by most people** (a holiday view, a friend's portrait): must not be offered,
  whatever I'd do with it.

So the list is generous with keepers and strict with clutter, and a label that only says something about
one person's taste counts for nothing (**Unsure**, below).

## Always keep

Whatever else is in the photo:

- **A person as the subject**: a portrait, a selfie, friends, family, someone doing something. People in
  the background (customers in a shop, passers-by in a street) don't make it a keeper.
- **Children**.
- **Pets or animals**.
- **Celebrations and events**: birthdays, weddings, parties, concerts, games, trips out.
- **Travel and places**: views, landscapes, cityscapes, landmarks, a beach, a hotel balcony at sunset.
- **Art, museums and history**: paintings, sculptures, old buildings, a famous café's interior.
- **Things made, hobbies and keepsakes**: something you built or cooked for an occasion, a scanned old
  photo, a child's drawing.
- **A once-only moment**: anything that couldn't be photographed again.

## Always clutter

Photographed for a practical reason, worth nothing once that is done (the original exists, or the moment
has passed). Clutter wherever it is photographed: a receipt in Rome is still a receipt.

- Screenshots of passing information, and photos of screens.
- Receipts, invoices, documents, letters and forms photographed for reference; cards and IDs.
- Tickets and passes, timetables, information signs photographed to read later.
- Where something was left: a parking space or number, a locker, a parked car to find again.
- Labels, barcodes, QR codes, model and serial numbers; meter readings and warning lights.
- Things to fix, to buy, to sell or to show someone; groceries and food to use up; parcels.
- Flat viewings and moving; menus; whiteboards and slides; notes.
- Memes and forwarded images; accidental shots (a pocket, a blur, black).

## Matter of taste

Neither rule settles them, and people differ: some keep every one, some none.

- Meals and drinks, cafés, restaurants and bars.
- Shop windows, markets, shelves; signs, posters and street corners abroad.
- Interiors, furniture or objects someone liked; cars.

The list doesn't offer these through a category that is clutter for almost everyone (groceries to use
up are clutter; a meal out isn't). Learning one person's taste is for later, per person, from what they
untick (milestone 6 in [cleanup-roadmap.md](cleanup-roadmap.md)), never for the shared list.

## When rules meet

- **A person as the subject wins.** A receipt held up to the camera is clutter; a friend holding a
  receipt and laughing is a keeper.
- **Practical purpose and a memory mixed**: matter of taste. A menu at a restaurant you loved, a ticket from a
  first concert.
- **Part of a view**: a street sign in a travel view is the view; a sign photographed to read it is
  clutter.

## Hard cases, settled

Photos the rules above left open, settled while labelling (2026-11-26). Each says which rule decides it.

- **Shop fronts, cafés and pubs seen from the street**: unsure (matter of taste: signs and street corners), not
  Signs. A sign is clutter only when it was photographed to read it.
- **Outfits and try-ons** (a T-shirt in a mirror, clothes held up): unsure. The person is there to show the clothes, and people differ on keeping them. A friend caught in a shop, not posing, not looking at the camera, is a portrait: keep (People). The test is who the photo is about, the clothes or the person.
- **Evidence of damage from long ago** (a crashed car for the insurance, a dent): unsure. Practical once, a story now for some; the model can't tell an old accident from a repair still to book.
- **Traffic through the windscreen in a town**: clutter (Other). A commute, not a journey: there is no view, only the road and other cars. Open country from a car stays a journey (keep).
- **Workplaces** (an office kitchen, a corridor, a meeting room) with no one as the subject: clutter
  (Other). The team in the same kitchen is a keeper (a person as the subject wins).
- **A gross or funny close-up to show someone** (a plaster just ripped off): clutter. Nothing in it but
  the thing; the moment was the sending. An injury on the body (a grazed knee through torn jeans) is
  unsure: it is the person, not an object.
- **Work done at your home** (men digging up the front of the gate): unsure. It can matter for a claim
  years later, like evidence of damage.
- **A bug on a wall** (a fly, a spider): clutter. Taken to identify it or to complain; not an animal photo.
  (Settled loosely: the labeller kept two as "an animal", without feeling strongly.)
- **A slice of life with strangers, taken to send a friend** (a queue at a fast-food counter): unsure.
  Strangers don't make it a keeper, but there is a moment and a place in it.
- **Close-ups in a museum** (a relief, a carved tablet, a meteorite's label): keep (art, museums and
  history). Out of context they look like a wall or a sign; label them for what they are. So is a poor shot of
  a ruin (an amphitheatre's stones): a bad photo of a historic place is still one.
- **Modern and industrial interiors** (a museum's hall, an atrium, a converted warehouse): keep
  (Architecture and interiors). An empty flat, an office or a coworking space is not one of them, nor a
  shopping centre, even on a trip: the category is for interiors that are striking in themselves. A trip
  photo that looks like nothing (a mall kiosk) is unsure.
- **A landmark seen past a meal** (a church and its square over a bar table): keep (Views, scenery and
  landmarks). The meal makes it less of a keeper, but offering it for the trash would still be wrong.

Choosing between keep and unsure, ask only that: would offering it for the trash be wrong? The app does one of
two things with a photo, offers it or not, so keep means "never offer", clutter "offer", unsure "either is fine".
There is no second choice to give: "keep, or at worst unsure" is keep. Likewise "a keep category, or at worst a
neutral one" (a landmark over a bar table landing in Restaurants, cafés and bars): a keep label is met by both,
as neither offers it; only being offered counts against it. Its kind says which keep category should have saved
it if it was offered.
- **Meals and drinks**: unsure, as the rules say, however often they were trashed before; in a café too
  (a latte, cake, a croissant by the window), even the best cake you ever had: only you know that. Something
  you cooked or baked is unsure too: some keep it as proof they can cook. It is the finished dish that is
  unsure; raw ingredients being prepared (chicken being cut up) are clutter. Food stored or bought (groceries on
  the counter, a tub held up, a pack of bread) is Food to buy or use up: clutter. A bottle of wine held up
  is a drink: unsure.
- **Cafés, bars and lounges inside**: with your people as the subject, keep (People). Empty, or with
  strangers only, unsure: whether a room is stylish is taste. A modern or industrial building (a museum's
  hall, a converted warehouse) is Architecture and interiors (keep); a café is not one just for its bare
  pipes.
- **Which keep category**: when several fit (a friend in the sea on holiday: People, Nature, Views,
  Adventures), pick the one most plainly true. Scores count only keep, clutter or unsure.
- **From a trip, or with your people in it**: keep, unless it was taken for a practical reason. A plain
  street or courtyard abroad, a city at night, a ferry deck, a plane's aisle: a fair share keeps these.
  Unsure is for photos people really differ on, not for ones that are merely dull.
- **Set up to show or sell** (an empty shop, ornaments on a shelf, a hotel room, a ring held up, tools laid
  out): clutter (Things to buy, sell or show). A thing someone made (a clay model, a pie) is Hobbies and
  things made: keep. The purpose decides, not the price: a car shot for a sale listing is clutter too; a car
  at home for any other reason is taste (and a listing photo that is now a memory, like a late parent's car,
  is unsure: only the owner knows). Worn, it is a look (a watch on a wrist, painted nails): unsure, like outfits.
- **Goods in a shop** (a shelf of bottles, a snack abroad, a clothes rail): clutter (Products), even on a
  trip. A sign close up (a pharmacy's, a laundrette's) is Signs: clutter; the shop front with its street
  around it stays unsure.
- **Pets in the dark** (a black cat in a night window): keep. A thumbnail hides them; label the photo you
  see full size.

Label what the photo is, as its owner knows it: the owner reviews the suggestions and remembers the
museum around a close-up. Where only the owner's story makes it a keeper (an old accident, a joke between
friends), it is unsure.

## Labelling

On the developer page, **Space** (or **K**) keep, **Delete** clutter (**Enter** confirms the highlighted kind), each then with the category that
should have decided it (a keep category after Keep, a clutter one after Clutter; **o** Other when none fits),
**U** unsure, also for a matter of taste: "I'd keep (or trash) this, but many people wouldn't". Answer for most
people, not your own taste: Clutter only when almost anyone would trash it, Keep when a fair share would keep
it, Unsure when people really differ. Unsure counts for nothing in the scores. (Until 2026-11-23 taste had
its own key, **T**; those labels still count as taste, which also counts for nothing.) Clutter labelled
with a kind the rules call taste counts as taste too (`TASTE_KINDS` in `clutterLabels.ts`: Food and drink,
for meals and drinks labelled clutter before these rules; decided 2026-11-22).

Labels are one person's view. When a label conflicts with the rules above, the agent doesn't overrule it
silently: it shows the photo and the rule, and the labeller decides whether it was taste (T) or a slip.

## How the list is judged

- **Precision** (of the photos offered, the share that is clutter): **at least 95%**, on photos labelled at
  random from the whole library (**Label random photos…**).
- **Recall** (of the clutter there is, the share offered): as high as precision allows; 50% first, then 60%.
- **Each category, at its edge**: at most 1 wrong among its riskiest 24 photos, with at least 10 of them
  labelled; otherwise it doesn't ship yet. Before a category ships, its wrong photos are looked at: a face
  or a once-only moment stops it, whatever the numbers.
- **A second library**: before the list ships, someone else labels a few hundred random photos of their
  own, and precision is checked there too.
